The Midterms

Halfway through a president's four years, American voters get to say how it is going. Scroll to see what they voted for, what happened, and who one of the new governors is.

Jan 2025Term starts
Nov 2026Midterms
Jan 2029Term ends

Midterm elections happen in the middle of the president's term. The president is not on the ballot. Voters choose people for Congress and for their state. The result is a kind of report card, and it decides how much power the president's party has for the last two years.

A new Democratic governor

Keisha Lance Bottoms, Georgia

Keisha Lance Bottoms, governor-elect of Georgia.

A former mayor of Atlanta who will become the first Black woman elected governor in US history.

  • BackgroundBorn in 1970 and raised in Atlanta. Her father was the singer Major Lance.
  • CareerLawyer. Mayor of Atlanta from 2018 to 2022. Later worked for the Democratic National Committee and in President Biden's White House.
  • The raceShe beat Republican Rick Jackson, who spent more than $200 million of his own money. Her win ended Republican control of Georgia's state government.
  • NextShe is governor-elect. Brian Kemp stays in office until she takes over.

Two sides

What do they believe?

Tap a party to open it. These are short summaries, and people inside each party do not always agree.

RepublicansSmaller government, more personal freedom
  • Taxes: keep taxes low, so families and businesses keep more money.
  • Border: stronger border control and tougher immigration rules.
  • Energy: make more oil, gas and other energy at home.
  • Guns: strong protection of the right to own guns.
  • Schools: more choice for parents about where children study.
  • Values: many stress family, faith and tradition.
Supporters say: simple rules, more freedom and a strong, independent country.
Critics say: tax cuts can mostly help rich people and reduce public services, tough immigration rules can hurt families, and more oil and gas can add to climate change.
DemocratsGovernment should help people and share chances fairly
  • Health: make health care easier to get and cheaper.
  • Costs: lower everyday costs, with higher taxes on very rich people and big companies.
  • Rights: protect abortion rights and voting rights.
  • Climate: act on climate change and support clean energy.
  • Guns: stricter gun-safety laws, such as background checks.
  • Immigration: secure borders, and a legal path to citizenship for many people already in the US.
Supporters say: help for ordinary families, fairness, and protection of people's rights.
Critics say: higher taxes and more spending can slow the economy, more rules can limit choice and raise costs, and some programmes are very expensive.
